My research develops computer vision and deep learning methods for medical image analysis, with an emphasis on diagnosis and prognosis in pediatric neuro-oncology.

I am a research data analyst at the Center for Data-Driven Discovery in Biomedicine at Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, working with Dr. Anahita Fathi Kazerooni. My work spans multimodal survival modeling in pediatric low-grade glioma, segmentation-free differential diagnosis of posterior fossa tumors across multiple institutions, and multireader studies of how radiologists incorporate model output into diagnostic decisions. A methodological thread runs through all of it: quantifying predictive uncertainty, detecting out-of-distribution inputs, and allowing a model to abstain instead of returning an unreliable prediction.

I hold BSE and MSE degrees in Bioengineering from the University of Pennsylvania. My master’s thesis, on non-invasive and segmentation-free prediction of molecular subtypes in pediatric low-grade glioma, was selected for oral presentation at RSNA 2025. Before that I studied protein misfolding under oxidative stress in the Shorter Lab at the Perelman School of Medicine.

I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027. My interests are medical image analysis, multimodal and self-supervised representation learning, and reliable machine learning for clinical deployment. 01 Selected work

Uncertainty-Aware Risk Stratification in Pediatric Low-Grade Glioma

Individualized risk predictions are of limited clinical use without a sense of how stable they are. This work fuses deep learning features from a single T2-weighted MRI with molecular and clinical data across 360 imaging and 493 molecular subjects from the Children’s Brain Tumor Network, reaching a C-index up to 0.79 — and reports a per-patient confidence interval alongside every risk score, so a prediction the model is unsure about looks different from one it is sure about.

Published American Journal of Neuroradiology, 2026 · doi:10.3174/ajnr.A9550

Segmentation-Free Differential Diagnosis of Posterior Fossa Tumors

Most imaging pipelines require tumor segmentation, which is slow and rarely available at the point of decision. This model classifies directly from raw pre-operative MRI across 944 patients from three institutions, reaching up to 0.95 AUC-ROC and 87% accuracy on external validation. Crucially, it declines to predict on cases that fall outside its training distribution rather than producing a confident wrong answer.

Under review Co-first author · Neuro-Oncology, 2026

Uncertainty-Aware AI Assistance: A Multireader Study

Confidence estimates are only clinically meaningful if readers act on them. This multireader study measures how radiologists’ diagnostic accuracy changes when they are shown model predictions with and without accompanying uncertainty estimates, together with an evaluation of the deployed workflow at an external site.

In preparation Co-first author · 2026

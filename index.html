Hi, I’m Fadel. I am a research data analyst at the Center for Data-Driven Discovery in Biomedicine at Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, where I work with Dr. Anahita Fathi Kazerooni on computer vision and deep learning for pediatric neuro-oncology.

I am broadly interested in imaging models that a clinician can act on. A model that is accurate on average is not the same as a model that is useful for the patient in front of you, and most of what I work on sits in that gap:

  • Uncertainty-aware prediction. Getting a model to express how far an individual prediction can be trusted, and to abstain when the evidence does not support one, instead of reporting a single accuracy figure over a whole test set.
  • Multimodal representation learning. How imaging should be combined with molecular, histologic, and clinical data when the modalities are incomplete, unevenly informative, and available for different subsets of patients.
  • Robustness under distribution shift. What a model does when it meets a post-treatment scan, a degraded acquisition, or a histology absent from training, and how those cases can be recognized before a prediction is released.
  • Agentic AI for medical image analysis. Using language models as an orchestration and verification layer around locked quantitative models, to decide whether a case is eligible for a prediction at all, while the diagnosis itself stays grounded in the model rather than in free-form generation.
  • Learning from small cohorts. Transfer learning and segmentation-free representations for pediatric data, where a few hundred subjects is a large study and annotation is expensive.
  • Human-AI collaboration. Whether uncertainty-aware output changes what a clinician concludes, measured in reader studies rather than benchmark tables.

I would also like to work beyond MRI, particularly in digital pathology, where the same questions apply to a very different kind of image.

Previously I completed my BSE and MSE in Bioengineering at the University of Pennsylvania, where my master’s thesis on segmentation-free molecular subtyping of pediatric low-grade glioma was selected for oral presentation at RSNA 2025. Before that I worked on TDP-43 misfolding under oxidative stress in the Shorter Lab at the Perelman School of Medicine. I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027.

01 Publications

† Equal contribution.

  1. Batal, F., et al. “Uncertainty-Aware Risk Stratification in Pediatric Low-Grade Glioma Using Multimodal Data.” American Journal of Neuroradiology, July 2026.

    Deep learning features from a single T2-weighted MRI, fused with molecular subtype and clinical variables, to predict progression-free survival. Every risk score carries a per-patient confidence interval, and late fusion with the molecular model narrowed those intervals by about 75%.

    Published doi:10.3174/ajnr.A9550

  2. Kazerooni, A.F.†, Batal, F.†, et al. “PedDx: An Uncertainty-Aware Deep Learning Framework for Pediatric Posterior Fossa Tumor Diagnosis With Agentic AI-Enabled Independent External Evaluation.”

    A segmentation-free classifier for five posterior fossa histologies, trained on skull-stripped T2-weighted MRI across three institutions. It calibrates its probabilities, abstains when no class clears its threshold, and is evaluated under real distribution shift, including post-treatment imaging, artifact, and rare histologies withheld from training. An agentic layer around the locked model decides whether a case is eligible for a diagnosis at all; a collaborating team in Australia ran the framework independently and reached 81% accuracy.

    In preparation Co-first author · 2026

  3. Kazerooni, A.F.†, Batal, F.†, et al. “Uncertainty-Aware AI Assistance for Pediatric Posterior Fossa Tumor Diagnosis: A Multireader Study.”

    A paired study in which neuroradiologists diagnose the same cases before and after seeing model output, so each reader is their own control. It asks whether an explicit uncertainty estimate changes how much weight readers give a prediction, and when assistance helps rather than hurts.

    In preparation Co-first author · 2026

  4. Vunnava, B., Lu, M., Batal, F., et al. “Atlas-Based MRI Spatial Phenotyping in Pediatric Low-Grade Glioma.”

    Probabilistic mapping of tumor location into a common atlas space, related to developmental, molecular, and surgical outcome variables.

    In preparation 2026

  5. An, K., Batal, F., Svyatskaya, S., Nguyen, V. “Gene Therapy as a Promising Approach for the Underlying Causes of Autism Spectrum Disorder.” Berkeley Pharma Tech Journal of Medicine 3, no. 1 (2023): 10-25.

    An undergraduate review of the genetic basis of autism spectrum disorder and the gene therapy approaches being explored against it.

    Published doi:10.52243/bptjm.v1i4.38

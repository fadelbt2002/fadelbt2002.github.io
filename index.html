My research develops computer vision and deep learning methods for medical image analysis, with an emphasis on diagnosis and prognosis in pediatric neuro-oncology.

I am a research data analyst at the Center for Data-Driven Discovery in Biomedicine at Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, working with Dr. Anahita Fathi Kazerooni. I use machine learning and deep learning methods from computer vision for medical image analysis in pediatric neuro-oncology. My current projects are multimodal survival modeling in pediatric low-grade glioma, segmentation-free differential diagnosis of posterior fossa tumors across multiple institutions, and multireader studies of how radiologists use model output in diagnosis.

What interests me is not model performance on its own but how these models should be built and combined with the rest of the data a patient generates, including molecular, histologic, and clinical information, so that they answer translational questions: what this tumor is, how it is likely to behave, and what that means for the decision a physician has to make. Part of that is how a model should represent what it does not know. A prediction a clinician can act on has to carry its own uncertainty, recognize inputs that fall outside the distribution it was trained on, and abstain when the evidence is insufficient. I would also like to work beyond MRI, particularly in digital pathology, where the same questions apply to a very different kind of image.

I hold BSE and MSE degrees in Bioengineering from the University of Pennsylvania. My master’s thesis, on non-invasive and segmentation-free prediction of molecular subtypes in pediatric low-grade glioma, was selected for oral presentation at RSNA 2025. Before that I studied protein misfolding under oxidative stress in the Shorter Lab at the Perelman School of Medicine.

I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027, with interests in medical image analysis, multimodal and self-supervised representation learning, and machine learning that clinicians can act on. 01 Selected work

Uncertainty-Aware Multimodal Survival Modeling in Pediatric Low-Grade Glioma

Pediatric low-grade gliomas are the most common central nervous system tumors in children, and their management is a balance between disease control and lifelong treatment morbidity. Outcomes diverge sharply with resectability and tumor biology. Gross total resection is associated with ten-year progression-free survival above 85%, while deep-seated or midline tumors that require adjuvant therapy fall to roughly 50%. Molecular subtype is a principal prognostic determinant under the WHO CNS5 classification. KIAA1549-BRAF fusion is generally favorable, and BRAF V600E is unfavorable, particularly alongside CDKN2A deletion.

Radiomic approaches improve risk stratification over clinical variables alone, but they depend on tumor segmentation and multiparametric acquisition, which limits how well they scale across institutions. Like most survival frameworks, they also report individualized risk as a point estimate that implies more precision than the data support. This study asks whether transfer-learned deep features from a single T2-weighted sequence can replace that pipeline, and what molecular subtype adds once clinical and imaging information are already in the model. Risk scores from a clinico-imaging model and a clinical-molecular model are combined by late fusion, and bootstrap resampling attaches a confidence interval to every patient’s prediction.

Published American Journal of Neuroradiology, 2026 · doi:10.3174/ajnr.A9550

Segmentation-Free Differential Diagnosis of Pediatric Posterior Fossa Tumors

Posterior fossa tumors are the largest category of childhood brain tumors. The major entities are medulloblastoma, ependymoma, atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumor, low-grade glioma, and diffuse midline glioma. They share an anatomical compartment but differ in biology, prognosis, and treatment, so the suspected diagnosis before surgery shapes how aggressively a tumor is resected, whether biopsy or upfront multimodal therapy is appropriate, and how families are counseled. An incorrect pre-operative impression can lead to unnecessary surgery, delayed treatment, or avoidable complications such as posterior fossa syndrome. These tumors nonetheless overlap substantially in MRI appearance even for experienced neuroradiologists, and subspecialty pediatric neuroradiology is not available at every center.

This work develops a segmentation-free classifier that operates on skull-stripped T2-weighted MRI alone, removing the manual delineation step that limits most pediatric imaging pipelines. The broader question is what evidence a diagnostic model owes a clinician beyond an accuracy figure. The framework addresses that through probability calibration, an abstention rule that returns no prediction when no class clears its threshold, and a systematic out-of-distribution analysis. That analysis covers clinical shifts such as post-operative and post-treatment imaging, artifact, and tumors extending beyond the posterior fossa, as well as biological shifts from rare histologies withheld from training. These are the conditions usually excluded from curated test sets and unavoidable in deployment.

Under review Co-first author · Neuro-Oncology, 2026

Uncertainty-Aware AI Assistance: A Multireader Study and External Workflow Evaluation

Showing that a diagnostic model is accurate is not the same as showing that it helps. Two questions remain once a model is locked. The first is whether access to its output, and to the uncertainty attached to that output, changes what a radiologist concludes. The second is whether the surrounding system can be operated safely somewhere it was not built. This study pairs a multireader experiment, in which neuroradiologists diagnose the same posterior fossa cases before and after seeing model output, with an external deployment at a collaborating institution abroad.

The deployment arm treats safety as a property of the workflow rather than of the model. An auditable orchestration layer defines an intended-use boundary and enforces it in stages: input and metadata validation, technical quality control, verification of preprocessing, anatomical localization, inference under the locked model, then uncertainty and out-of-distribution assessment. Each case ends in one of four dispositions, which are release, release with caution, retry, and abstain and escalate, each with a recorded rationale. Orchestration is kept separate from interpretation, so the diagnostic decision stays grounded in locked quantitative tools rather than unconstrained language-model inference. The evaluation asks whether in-distribution gains survive contact with a real case mix that includes studies the model should decline.

Oxidative Stress and TDP-43 Aggregation earlier work

TDP-43 is an RNA-binding protein whose mislocalization and aggregation is the defining neuropathology of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and is also observed in frontotemporal dementia, LATE, and Alzheimer’s disease. Oxidative stress is thought to drive that process rather than accompany it, but the two are usually studied separately. This project imposed a defined oxidative environment in vitro using oxidized glutathione and measured its effect on TDP-43 aggregation kinetics and liquid-liquid phase separation. It then tested whether a short RNA bait known to bind TDP-43 and suppress its aggregation keeps that protective effect under oxidizing conditions.

Undergraduate research Shorter Lab, Perelman School of Medicine · 2022-2024

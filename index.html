Hi, I’m Fadel! I’m a Research Data Analyst at the Center for Data-Driven Discovery in Biomedicine (D3B) at Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, where I work with Dr. Anahita Fathi Kazerooni.

My research interests are in trustworthy AI for pediatric neuro-oncology. I currently work on:

  • Transfer learning for translational applications in medical imaging in pediatric brain tumors, including risk stratification and differential diagnosis
  • Uncertainty quantification, so a prediction carries a measure of how far it can be trusted
  • An agentic AI layer that orchestrates preprocessing, quality control, and inference as a single end-to-end framework

I’m increasingly interested in self-supervised, multimodal foundation models that learn jointly across imaging, digital pathology, and molecular data, and in reader studies that test whether a method changes what a clinician concludes, not just where it lands on a benchmark.

I have been working with Dr. Fathi Kazerooni for about two years, first as a master’s student at the University of Pennsylvania and now at D3B. I hold BSE and MSE degrees in bioengineering from Penn, where my thesis was on non-invasive molecular subtyping of pediatric low-grade gliomas. Earlier, as an undergraduate, I worked in James Shorter’s lab on protein misfolding.

I’m always happy to talk research. Feel free to reach out at batalf@chop.edu.

  1. Uncertainty-Aware Risk Stratification in Pediatric Low-Grade Glioma Using Multimodal Data

    Fadel Batal, et al.

    American Journal of Neuroradiology, 2026

    Deep learning features extracted from a single T2-weighted MRI are combined with molecular subtype and clinical variables to predict progression-free survival, using 360 subjects with imaging and 493 with genomic profiling from the Children’s Brain Tumor Network. The clinico-imaging model reached Harrell’s C-indices of 0.73 in discovery and 0.70 in replication, statistically comparable to a multiparametric radiomic pipeline while requiring neither tumor segmentation nor multiple sequences. Late fusion with a clinical-molecular model improved replication performance (0.68 vs 0.63) and reclassified 18 patients in a manner consistent with established BRAF-associated prognostic biology. Bootstrap resampling gives every patient a confidence interval on their risk score, and fusion narrowed the median width by about 75%.

    Published DOI Code

  2. PedDx: An Uncertainty-Aware Deep Learning Framework for Pediatric Posterior Fossa Tumor Diagnosis With Agentic AI-Enabled Independent External Evaluation

    Anahita Fathi Kazerooni†, Fadel Batal†, et al.

    In preparation, 2026

    Pre-operative differential diagnosis of pediatric posterior fossa tumors is critical for surgical planning, treatment stratification, and clinical decision-making. We developed a segmentation-free deep learning framework to classify pediatric posterior fossa tumors and evaluated its performance across in-distribution and out-of-distribution (OOD) cohorts. Building on this locked model, we developed and externally evaluated an auditable AI workflow designed to execute the model, identify inappropriate or unreliable inputs, recover from remediable failures, and prevent unsupported diagnoses from being released. Together, these analyses test whether AI advice is useful when applied within scope and whether workflow-level safeguards can govern when that advice should be delivered.

    In preparation Co-first author

  3. Uncertainty-Aware AI Assistance for Pediatric Posterior Fossa Tumor Diagnosis: A Multireader Study

    Anahita Fathi Kazerooni†, Fadel Batal†, et al.

    In preparation, 2026

    A reader study asking not only whether accuracy moves when neuroradiologists are shown model output, but under what conditions assistance helps or harms: whether readers defer to confident errors, and whether an explicit uncertainty estimate changes how much weight they give a prediction.

Marhaba (مرحباً)!

A model that is accurate on average can still fail badly on the patient in front of you. I build imaging models for pediatric brain tumors that report how much to trust them.

I am a research data analyst at the Center for Data-Driven Discovery in Biomedicine at Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, working with Anahita Fathi Kazerooni on survival modeling and differential diagnosis from MRI. Clinical imaging models are usually evaluated on how often they are right; I am more interested in whether they know when they are wrong. My work builds risk stratification and diagnostic models that quantify their own confidence, flag out-of-distribution cases, and abstain rather than guess — and then tests whether clinicians actually use that information.

I hold a BSE and an MSE in Bioengineering from the University of Pennsylvania, where my master’s thesis on segmentation-free molecular subtyping of pediatric low-grade glioma became an oral presentation at RSNA 2025. Before that I worked in the Shorter Lab at the Perelman School of Medicine on protein misfolding under oxidative stress.

I am originally from Syria, and I am looking to start a PhD in 2027.

Selected work

Uncertainty-Aware Risk Stratification in Pediatric Low-Grade Glioma

Individualized risk predictions are of limited clinical use without a sense of how stable they are. This work fuses deep learning features from a single T2-weighted MRI with molecular and clinical data across 360 imaging and 493 molecular subjects from the Children’s Brain Tumor Network, reaching a C-index up to 0.79 — and reports a per-patient confidence interval alongside every risk score, so a prediction the model is unsure about looks different from one it is sure about.

Published in American Journal of Neuroradiology, 2026 · doi:10.3174/ajnr.A9550

Segmentation-Free Differential Diagnosis of Posterior Fossa Tumors

Most imaging pipelines require tumor segmentation, which is slow and rarely available at the point of decision. This model classifies directly from raw pre-operative MRI across 944 patients from three institutions, reaching up to 0.95 AUC-ROC and 87% accuracy on external validation. Crucially, it declines to predict on cases that fall outside its training distribution rather than producing a confident wrong answer.

Co-first author · Under review at Neuro-Oncology, 2026

Does Uncertainty-Aware AI Actually Help Readers?

A model that quantifies its own confidence is only useful if clinicians act on that information. This multireader study evaluates how radiologists’ diagnostic accuracy changes when they are shown model predictions with and without uncertainty estimates, alongside an evaluation of the deployed workflow at an external site.

Co-first author · In preparation, 2026

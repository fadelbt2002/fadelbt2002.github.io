Hi, I’m Fadel! I’m a Research Data Analyst at the Center for Data-Driven Discovery in Biomedicine (D3B) at Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, where I work with Dr. Anahita Fathi Kazerooni.

My research interests are in trustworthy AI for pediatric neuro-oncology. I currently work on:

  • Transfer learning for translational applications in medical imaging in pediatric brain tumors, including risk stratification and differential diagnosis
  • Uncertainty quantification, so a prediction carries a measure of how far it can be trusted
  • An agentic AI layer that orchestrates preprocessing, quality control, and inference as a single end-to-end framework

I’m increasingly interested in self-supervised, multimodal foundation models that learn jointly across imaging, digital pathology, and molecular data, and in reader studies that test whether a method changes what a clinician concludes, not just where it lands on a benchmark.

Before D3B, I studied bioengineering at Penn (BSE, MSE), where I worked in the Shorter Lab on protein misfolding and completed a thesis on non-invasive molecular subtyping of pediatric low-grade gliomas.

I’m always happy to talk research. Feel free to reach out at batalf@chop.edu.

01 Publications

† Equal contribution.

  1. Batal, F., et al. “Uncertainty-Aware Risk Stratification in Pediatric Low-Grade Glioma Using Multimodal Data.” American Journal of Neuroradiology, July 2026.

    Deep learning features from a single T2-weighted MRI, fused with molecular subtype and clinical variables, to predict progression-free survival. Every risk score carries a per-patient confidence interval, and late fusion with the molecular model narrowed those intervals by about 75%.

    Published doi:10.3174/ajnr.A9550

  2. Kazerooni, A.F.†, Batal, F.†, et al. “PedDx: An Uncertainty-Aware Deep Learning Framework for Pediatric Posterior Fossa Tumor Diagnosis With Agentic AI-Enabled Independent External Evaluation.”

    A segmentation-free classifier for five posterior fossa histologies, trained on skull-stripped T2-weighted MRI across three institutions. It calibrates its probabilities, abstains when no class clears its threshold, and is evaluated under real distribution shift, including post-treatment imaging, artifact, and rare histologies withheld from training. An agentic layer around the locked model decides whether a case is eligible for a diagnosis at all; a collaborating team in Australia ran the framework independently and reached 81% accuracy.

    In preparation Co-first author · 2026

  3. Kazerooni, A.F.†, Batal, F.†, et al. “Uncertainty-Aware AI Assistance for Pediatric Posterior Fossa Tumor Diagnosis: A Multireader Study.”

    A paired study in which neuroradiologists diagnose the same cases before and after seeing model output, so each reader is their own control. It asks whether an explicit uncertainty estimate changes how much weight readers give a prediction, and when assistance helps rather than hurts.

    In preparation Co-first author · 2026

  4. Vunnava, B., Lu, M., Batal, F., et al. “Atlas-Based MRI Spatial Phenotyping in Pediatric Low-Grade Glioma.”

    Probabilistic mapping of tumor location into a common atlas space, related to developmental, molecular, and surgical outcome variables.

    In preparation 2026

  5. An, K., Batal, F., Svyatskaya, S., Nguyen, V. “Gene Therapy as a Promising Approach for the Underlying Causes of Autism Spectrum Disorder.” Berkeley Pharma Tech Journal of Medicine 3, no. 1 (2023): 10-25.

    An undergraduate review of the genetic basis of autism spectrum disorder and the gene therapy approaches being explored against it.

    Published doi:10.52243/bptjm.v1i4.38
